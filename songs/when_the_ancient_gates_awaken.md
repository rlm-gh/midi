# When the Ancient Gates Awaken

## Original brief

Create a more complex orchestral arrangement in MIDI 1.0 for the title screen
of a medieval fantasy computer RPG. Begin calmly, then introduce instruments
gradually until the music becomes an epic theme.

## Embellishment

The title screen opens on a ruined royal gateway above a valley of sleeping
villages. Fog moves between the stones; the first light has not yet reached
the banners. The music should suggest a world with a long memory and an
adventure still waiting for its first step. Begin almost privately: a low harp
fifth, widely spaced upper notes, and enough silence to hear the imagined
hall around them. A recorder enters alone with a small, memorable call. A
nylon-string guitar, standing in for a lute, answers quietly. Warm cellos
arrive beneath the last part of the introduction.

Write an original 104-measure orchestral title theme, approximately four
minutes forty-eight seconds including its release, in a spacious 4/4. Its home
is E minor, with occasional Dorian C-sharps giving the pastoral passages an
older, brighter colour. The main idea begins with the upward fourth B–E,
continues through F-sharp to G, and answers by falling toward B. Make this
recognisable enough to survive changes of register, rhythm, and instrument.
The early statement belongs to the recorder and then the oboe. Give wind
players breaths and let the accompaniment answer in those spaces.

The orchestra should gather through audible stages. Violas first supply a
quiet inner line; contrabasses establish weight; second violins turn the
plucked pulse into short bowed eighth notes. Horns carry a broader version of
the principal idea, and first violins answer above them. Harmony should move
through inversions, descending basses, suspensions that resolve within the
measure, and genuine dominant-to-tonic arrivals. Keep the leading melody
clear when the texture grows.

At the middle of the piece, reveal the wider kingdom. Move into G major and
introduce a second, more flowing theme in the violins: longer arcs, a higher
register, and an answering flute. Add a wordless choir and restrained brass
replies. This is a first view of grandeur, with room left for a greater return.
Use timpani and low drum strokes as structural weight, allowing snare rolls
and cymbal accents chiefly at the entrances to new phrases.

Then let a shadow cross the gate. Withdraw the choir, trumpets, and flute;
reduce the register, break the opening motif into short horn calls, and pass
answers through the low strings and oboe. Rebuild over a dominant pedal with
accelerating bowed figures and a measured percussion crescendo. A brief,
shared breath before the return should make the arrival feel earned.

Bring back the main theme in first violins and horns, now broad and assured.
Under it, give the cellos a separately composed countermelody, rather than
simply doubling the bass. Second violins continue the motion while violas
hold the middle of the harmony. Add flute descants, choir, low brass, and
short trumpet fanfares in successive phrases. At the final high point, turn
the principal idea into E major: G-sharp replaces the earlier G, and the
gate opens into sunlight. Let this eight-measure coronation have the fullest
register and strongest dynamics of the piece, with a clear final cadence.

After the orchestral arrival, gradually release its weight. Recall the
recorder's original call over harp and lute, retaining a little of the new
major-key warmth before resting on an open E–B fifth. Leave a release measure
for the sound to decay. The ending should invite the player to begin an
adventure and allow a playlist to return naturally to the misty opening;
this file has a composed ending, not an embedded seamless-loop instruction.

Shape the performance with phrase swells, continuous expression changes on
sustained instruments, distinct articulations, a restrained tempo curve,
and small reproducible timing and velocity variations. Use General MIDI
instruments on independent channels, reserve channel 10 for unpitched
percussion, and include a conductor track with tempo, meter, key changes,
and section markers. Deliver both the MIDI and its editable Python score.
